Which Retroid Pocket Should You Buy in 2026? Pocket 6 vs Pocket 5 vs Nova vs Classic

Short answer

For most people the Retroid Pocket 6 is the best all-round pick: a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 chip and a 5.5-inch 120 Hz AMOLED screen, listed from $244 on the official store. If PS2 and GameCube are your ceiling and you want to spend less, the Pocket 5 was listed at $179 (reduced from $209). The Pocket Classic ($99) suits small, Game Boy-style 2D gaming.

Retroid makes several Android handhelds that look similar in photos but differ a lot in power and size. This guide compares the four most-asked-about models using the official store and independent reviews. We have not tested these devices ourselves.

Which Retroid Pocket is best overall?

The Pocket 6 is the strongest of the four on paper. Independent catalog data lists a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 chip, a 5.5-inch 1920x1080 AMOLED panel with 120 Hz refresh and a 6,000 mAh battery. One review of the Pocket 5 says the Pocket 6 adds faster processing and Switch capability, with the usual caution that results vary by game and settings.

One third-party performance index scores the Pocket 6 at 90 for GameCube, 88 for Nintendo 3DS, 87 for Wii, 70 for Sega Saturn and 67 for Wii U. Treat these as relative guides, not guarantees.

Is the Retroid Pocket 5 still worth buying?

It can be, if you find one at a good price. The Pocket 5 uses a Snapdragon 865, a 5.5-inch AMOLED screen, hall-effect sticks and active cooling. A hands-on review calls it the cheapest way to get an 865 behind a 5.5-inch AMOLED screen and rates NES through GBA, PS1, N64, Dreamcast and PSP as excellent, GameCube as good and PS2 as adequate, with demanding titles struggling. Its weaknesses, according to the same review, are weak speakers, a D-pad position that can tire your thumb, and a size that is hard to pocket. The review also describes the Pocket 5 as discontinued, so supply is limited.

Who should pick the Nova or the Classic?

Choose the Pocket Nova if you want similar 8 Gen 2 power in a smaller horizontal body with a 4:3 screen, which suits older games. Choose the Pocket Classic if you mainly play Game Boy, SNES and PS1-era games and want a compact vertical device. It has no analog sticks, so 3D games are not its strength.

What to check before you buy

  • Stock and shipping: the official store showed every model as sold out when we checked, so confirm availability and delivery times.
  • Games you plan to play: match the device to your most demanding system (PS2, GameCube, 3DS or Switch), not your average one.
  • Storage and RAM: configurations vary, so check the exact variant on the product page.
  • Your games: emulators are legal in many places, but downloading copyrighted game files is not. Use games you own and check the rules where you live.

FAQ

Is the Retroid Pocket 6 worth it?

For players who want PS2, GameCube and 3DS in one handheld, yes: it has the fastest chip of the four models compared here, a 120 Hz AMOLED screen and a large battery, from about $244.

Can the Retroid Pocket 5 play PS2 games?

A review rates PS2 on the Pocket 5 as adequate: most games are playable, but demanding titles such as God of War struggle.

What is the cheapest Retroid Pocket?

Of the models we checked, the Pocket Classic is the lowest at $99. The Pocket Flip 2 and Duo Lite start at $149.

Is the Retroid Pocket Nova better than the Pocket 6?

It has the same Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 chip in a smaller 4.5-inch body with a 4:3 screen. The Pocket 6 has a larger 5.5-inch 16:9 display, so the better choice depends on the games you play and how much you value size.

Where can I buy a Retroid Pocket?

Retroid sells its handhelds through its official store. Prices and stock change often, so check the listing before you order.
