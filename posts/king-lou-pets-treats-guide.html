Is raw dog food safe?

Raw pet food carries real handling risks. In an FDA study of more than 1,000 pet food samples collected in 2010 to 2012, 15 of 196 raw food samples tested positive for Salmonella and 32 for Listeria monocytogenes, and raw food was more likely to be contaminated than other types. The FDA says the risk applies to pets and to the people who handle the food.

To reduce the risk, the FDA recommends that you:

  • Wash your hands for at least 20 seconds after handling raw pet food.
  • Clean and disinfect surfaces, bowls and utensils that touch it, using hot soapy water followed by a bleach solution.
  • Keep raw food frozen until use and thaw it in the refrigerator or microwave.
  • Avoid rinsing raw meat, which can splash bacteria.
  • Keep raw food separate from other foods and refrigerate leftovers promptly.
  • Avoid letting your pet lick your face after feeding raw food.

Young children, older adults, pregnant people and people with weakened immune systems are at higher risk. Because every dog is different, it is sensible to talk to your own veterinarian before adding raw treats or food to your dog's diet.
